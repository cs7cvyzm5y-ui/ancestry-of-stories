# Research pass: direct influence versus mediated transmission

## Question

The project began with a strong interest in author-to-author influence. As the corpus grows, a second hypothesis has become increasingly plausible:

> Creative ancestry may often be better explained by **paths through works, translations, institutions, traditions, media forms, and historical worlds** than by a single author-to-author arrow.

This pass deliberately tested both models rather than assuming either was correct.

## Result so far

The evidence does **not** support abandoning direct influence. Several unusually clean direct relationships exist.

### Direct transmission is real

**Kafka → García Márquez**

García Márquez explicitly described reading *The Metamorphosis* as a formative event: Kafka's opening showed him that fiction was allowed to state an impossible event with complete matter-of-factness. This is strong work-to-person transmission.

Source:
https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3196/the-art-of-fiction-no-69-gabriel-garcia-marquez

**Chinua Achebe → Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o**

Ngũgĩ recalled that Achebe read part of the manuscript that became *Weep Not, Child*, offered useful suggestions, and spoke about it to his Heinemann publishers. This is strong person-to-person creative and institutional support.

Source:
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0021909613506456

These cases justify keeping people as first-class nodes.

## But direct arrows are often incomplete explanations

### Translation as mediator

A more accurate García Márquez path is:

```text
Joyce — Ulysses
      ↓
José Salas Subirat's 1945 Spanish translation
      ↓
Gabriel García Márquez
```

García Márquez says he read the Spanish edition available to him. Modeling the translation prevents the graph from pretending that cultural transmission occurred without language, publishing, and edition history.

Sources:
https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3196/the-art-of-fiction-no-69-gabriel-garcia-marquez
https://cvc.cervantes.es/trujaman/anteriores/noviembre_16/23112016.htm

### Publishing institution as mediator

Achebe's relationship to later African writers is also larger than a simple personal arrow:

```text
Chinua Achebe
      ↓ editorial support
Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o

Chinua Achebe
      ↓ editorial adviser
Heinemann African Writers Series
      ↓ publishing network
Weep Not, Child and other African writing
```

The person-to-person relationship is real, but the African Writers Series helped scale transmission into a cross-border literary infrastructure.

Sources:
https://academic.oup.com/afraf/article-abstract/102/409/575/29533
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/ngugi-wa-thiongo-in-context/ngugi-and-the-african-writers-series/88EF2C5DE58AA1ABFC815FA216F65B73

### Historical practice becoming genre

The American Western is especially difficult to explain as a chain of famous authors.

A more useful path is:

```text
Mexican vaquero traditions ─┐
Black cowboy labor          ├→ western cattle labor
Indigenous cattle economies ┘
                                  ↓
                     frontier/cowboy representation
                                  ↑
dime novels → Wild West shows ────┘
                                  ↓
                         Western tradition
                                  ↓
                       Leone / later writers
                                  ↓
                         Stephen King
```

This does not make every historical participant a literary influence. It distinguishes **historical substrate** from **media mythmaking** and then from later textual transmission.

Sources:
https://www.nps.gov/grko/learn/historyculture/cowboys.htm
https://www.nps.gov/tapr/learn/historyculture/cowboys.htm
https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/geppi-gems/about-this-exhibition/westerns/

## Reaction is also often layered

Achebe's relationship to Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson* illustrates why a single influence arrow can mislead.

Achebe acknowledged the book as an important encounter and objected strongly to its representation of Nigeria. But he later resisted the simplified story that his own writing existed merely as a response to Cary.

The graph therefore records both:

- the specific Cary/Achebe encounter; and
- the broader colonial literary tradition that repeatedly positioned Africans as objects rather than narrators.

Source:
https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/entertainment/2008/03/09/things-fall-into-place/b2898372-de37-44d2-92ce-26f658763d2b/

## Media form can be more defensible than a famous comparison

For *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, reader comparisons to *The Running Man*, *Survivor*, and *Squid Game* are intuitively useful, but a clean primary-source statement identifying those specific works as origins has not yet been established.

Dinniman has, however, described the long-standing seed idea as an **"alien game show."**

The graph now represents:

```text
game-show / elimination format
            ↓
Dungeon Crawler Carl
```

while leaving *The Running Man* unconnected until stronger evidence appears.

Source:
https://www.iheart.com/podcast/1119-here-we-go-again-with-kal-300268750/episode/bonus-matt-dinniman-discusses-dungeon-crawler-340228290/

## Provisional conclusion

The initial author-influence hypothesis is **partly correct but too narrow**.

The emerging model is:

```text
WORLD / TRADITION / MEDIUM
           ↓
      transmission
           ↓
        PERSON
           ↓
       mediation
           ↓
         WORK
           ↓
publishers · translations · schools · adaptations · communities
           ↓
       later people
```

A direct author-to-author edge is valuable when supported. But the graph becomes more explanatory when it also asks:

- What work actually carried the idea?
- In what language or translation?
- Through which school, publisher, editor, or community?
- Was the later creator admiring, revising, or reacting against it?
- What historical system supplied the subject matter?
- Which part is cultural transmission and which part is merely shared context?

## Important limitation

This is a qualitative research result from a small, intentionally varied sample. It is **not** evidence that mediated transmission is statistically more common than direct author influence across literature as a whole.

Future corpus growth should keep testing the question rather than turning this provisional result into a new assumption.
