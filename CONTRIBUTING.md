# Contributing claims

The unit of contribution is a **claim**, not an opinion and not simply a line on a graph.

## Before adding an edge

Ask:
1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. Is this direct influence, indirect inheritance, context, reaction, adaptation, or only comparison?
3. Could the source plausibly precede and reach the target?
4. What evidence would falsify or weaken the claim?
5. Is the edge better represented through an intermediary node (translation, adaptation, teacher, movement, film, game, institution)?

## Minimum fields

- source ID
- target ID
- relation
- evidence class
- confidence (1–5)
- one-sentence note
- status (`verified` or `review`)
- supporting source URL for claims above comparison-only, unless the claim is basic metadata such as authorship

## Confidence rubric

**5** Explicit creator statement, direct adaptation/authorship, or comparably direct evidence.  
**4** Strong textual or scholarly evidence with little dispute.  
**3** Well-motivated lineage/context claim, but causal strength is diffuse.  
**2** Plausible inference worth investigating.  
**1** Comparison only. Never describe this as proven transmission.

## Source preference

1. Primary source: author interview, letter, diary, lecture, notebook, archival record.
2. Author estate / institutional archive / scholarly edition.
3. Peer-reviewed article or university-press scholarship.
4. High-quality biography or criticism.
5. Reliable journalism/interview.
6. Reference databases as discovery tools.

Do not use an unsourced wiki statement as the sole evidence for a substantive influence claim.

## Contested claims

Do not “resolve” a dispute by deleting one interpretation. Add the competing claim or counterevidence and label the relationship contested.

## Language

Prefer: “X explicitly cited Y,” “scholars have argued,” “the work reuses,” “the author had read,” or “Version 0 treats this as a comparison.”

Avoid: “X obviously stole from Y,” “this proves,” or biographical mind-reading.
