# Validation findings — 2026-09-16

The first hands-on validation pass covered The Dark Tower, Ernest Hemingway, The Giver, Dungeon Crawler Carl, One Hundred Years of Solitude, Things Fall Apart, and Edgar Allan Poe.

## Findings that appear structurally validated

- Typed graph is more useful than a family tree.
- Confidence filtering is understandable when paired with visible reasoning.
- `Why we think this` materially improves credibility and interpretability.
- Publication lineage, series sequence, authorship, influence, context, and comparison need separate relationship semantics.
- Person nodes are necessary to bridge lived/intellectual formation into authored works.
- Dense graphs require legends and stable line semantics.

## Findings requiring continued product work

- Add interval-aware dates and a timeline view.
- Separate Author Formation from Work Ancestry as interface lenses.
- Group multiple claims between the same source/target pair visually without collapsing them in the data.
- Add explicit textual-reference/intertext layer.
- Allow readers to propose candidate connections with their own reasoning and sources.
- Expand historical context so the graph is educational even when direct literary ancestry is sparse.
- Distinguish edges visible in the current graph from additional relationships listed in a selected node's detail panel.

## Research coverage finding

Dark Tower and Dungeon Crawler Carl are dense enough to test interaction, but Hemingway, Márquez, Achebe, and Poe reveal that the present seed corpus is too sparse for serious intellectual biography. The next phase should prioritize researched dossiers rather than broad node-count growth.
